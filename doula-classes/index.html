Words to know

Braxton Hicks
Practice contractions. They are irregular and stop with rest or water.
Baby drops
Baby moves lower. Breathing gets easier and labor is near.
Nesting
A sudden urge to clean and organize. Labor is near.
Mucus plug
Thick discharge that comes out before labor. Text the doula a photo.
Water breaking
A gush or trickle of clear fluid. Call the doula first.
Membrane sweep
A quick exam the doctor can do to help labor start.

Station is how low the baby's head is. Doctors only say it if asked.

Questions to ask the nurse

  1. "What's the baby's station?" at every check.
  2. "Can we wait upright a bit?" if it's 0 or lower.
  3. "Can we raise the Pitocin every 45 min instead of 30?"
  • Press the epidural button sparingly so Pavi can still move her legs.
  • She doesn't have to push every contraction. Resting between helps avoid tearing.
  • A burning feeling as the head comes out (the "ring of fire") means it's almost over.

Words to know

Epidural
Numbing medicine through a thin tube in the lower back. She stays awake and can still push.
Dilation
How open the cervix is, from 0 to 10 cm.
Effacement
How thin the cervix is, given as a percent. 100% is the goal.
Station
How low the baby's head is, from -3 (high) to +3 (crowning).
Pitocin
An IV drip that makes contractions stronger and more frequent.

Words to know

Colostrum
The thick, yellow first milk. Small amounts, full of antibodies.
Bilirubin
Too much turns the skin yellow (jaundice). Treated with a blue light.
Skin-to-skin
Baby lies bare on a parent's bare chest. Calms and warms the baby.
Postpartum floor
The recovery unit you move to after delivery.
