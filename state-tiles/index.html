State tiles

Seven maps of the United States, one small chart per state. Each tile is a 100% stacked area: the bands always add up to the whole, and time runs left to right. Gray means no data, usually because the place was not yet a state. The style copies 1POINT21 Interactive's "Where are Americans born?", which used IPUMS census microdata.

Hover or tap a tile to read the numbers for that year.

01How did each state vote?

Popular vote share for president, 1856 to 2024. Gray covers the years before statehood and the Civil War elections the seceded states sat out. Mississippi voted 97% Democratic in 1936 and 87% Republican in 1964. Vermont gave Republicans 78% in 1856 and Democrats 63% in 2024. In 1860 the Southern Democrats (Breckinridge) count as a third party, which turns the South light blue that year.

full sizedata (CSV)sources:Wikipedia state results tables, 1856-1972MIT Election Data and Science Lab, 1976-2024

02How does each state make electricity?

Share of net generation by fuel, 1990 to 2024. Coal supplied 86% of Iowa's power in 1990 and 21% in 2024, while clean sources rose from 13% to 66%. Kansas followed the same path. West Virginia still makes 85% of its power from coal. Delaware swapped coal for gas, and Massachusetts burns no coal at all.

full sizedata (CSV)source:EIA, net generation by state by energy source

03Who uses each state's energy?

Share of total energy consumed by sector, 1960 to 2024. Industry uses 70% of Louisiana's energy and 56% of Texas's. Transportation takes 59% in Hawaii. In DC, homes and offices now use 83% of the energy and industry 3%, down from 28% in 1960.

full sizedata (CSV)source:EIA State Energy Data System (SEDS)

04How does each state collect its taxes?

State government tax revenue by type, 1942 to 2025. A light blue band appears in the year a state adopts an income tax; Nebraska and Illinois had none in 1960 and over 40% of revenue from it by 2025. Texas and Nevada still have none, and Washington taxes only capital gains. Most of Alaska's orange is oil severance tax, 44% of its 2025 collections.

full sizedata (CSV)source:Census Bureau, Annual Survey of State Tax Collections

05How does each state earn a living?

Earnings by industry, 1929 to 2025. Farming was 42% of North Dakota's earnings in 1929 and 6% in 2025. Manufacturing was 48% of Michigan's earnings in 1950 and is 15% now. Connecticut went from 46% to 9%.

full sizedata (CSV)source:BEA, SAINC5 earnings by industry

06Who signs the paychecks?

Earnings by employer, 1929 to 2025. World War II shows up as a notch in nearly every tile: federal pay was 45% of Virginia's earnings in 1944 and 56% of DC's. In 1960 the federal government paid 43% of Alaska's earnings and 30% of Hawaii's. State and local government grew in most states after 1960.

full sizedata (CSV)source:BEA, SAINC5 earnings by industry

07Where does the money come from?

Personal income by source, 1929 to 2025. Government benefits such as Social Security, Medicare and Medicaid were 2% of income in most states in 1929. They are 31% in West Virginia and 28% in Mississippi today. Florida gets 32% of its income from dividends, interest and rent.

full sizedata (CSV)source:BEA, SAINC4 personal income by major component

--Notes and code

  • Every band is a share of the state's own total, so a tile shows mix and says nothing about size.
  • Presidential results before 1976 come from the state tables on each Wikipedia election page and agree with two independent R datasets (pscl, cluster) to within a point for most states. 1872 counts Greeley as the Democrat.
  • Clean electricity is nuclear, hydro, wind, solar, geothermal and biomass. Pumped storage and "other" are excluded.
  • BEA industry definitions change from SIC to NAICS in 2001. Negative farm earnings are set to zero, and a few suppressed values are interpolated.
  • State taxes exclude local taxes, which is why property tax looks small. DC has no state government and stays gray on that map.
