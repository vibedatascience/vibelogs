GEOGRAPHY

An archipelago, not just an island

  • Main island plus Vieques, Culebra, Mona, Desecheo, and Caja de Muertos. Only Vieques and Culebra are inhabited year-round.
  • Mostly mountainous: the Cordillera Central runs east–west, with flat coastal plains north and south and karst caves in the north.
  • Tectonic frontier: the island sits on the Caribbean–North American plate boundary. The Puerto Rico Trench, 71 mi north, plunges to 27,600 ft at the Milwaukee Deep, the deepest point in the Atlantic.
  • 17 man-made lakes and 50+ rivers, most born in the central mountains; northern rivers run longer and fuller than southern ones.
  • Tropical rainforest climate: about 85°F on the coasts, 70°F in the mountains, 66 inches of rain a year, hurricane season shared with the whole Caribbean.

HISTORY

Four thousand years in one line

~2000 BC
Ortoiroid hunter-gatherers settle the island; Saladoid farmers arrive around 500–200 BC, ancestors of the Taíno.
1493
Columbus arrives and names the island San Juan Bautista. Taíno society is devastated within a century by disease, exploitation, and war.
1508
Juan Ponce de León begins Spanish colonization. For 400 years the island is a fortified keystone of the Spanish Empire.
1898
Spanish–American War: the U.S. invades and Spain cedes Puerto Rico in the Treaty of Paris.
1917
Jones–Shafroth Act makes Puerto Ricans U.S. citizens; the Borinqueneers of the 65th Infantry later earn the Congressional Gold Medal.
1952
Commonwealth constitution takes effect on July 25; residents elect their own governor and legislature.
2017–20
Hurricanes Irma and Maria, the debt crisis, and a magnitude 6.4 earthquake reshape the island's economy and diaspora.

TOURISM

The comeback industry

  • Up to 10% of GDP comes from tourism, and hospitality anchors the wider services sector alongside finance and real estate.
  • Hurricane Maria shut the industry down in 2017 with damage estimated at $100 billion. By April 2019 life for visitors around the capital had largely returned to normal, and by late 2019 nearly every major destination was back in operation.
  • Discover Puerto Rico launched a 2018 campaign branding the island around its culture and history rather than generic Caribbean sun.
  • Gateway of the Caribbean: Luis Muñoz Marín International is the region's largest air hub, and the Port of San Juan is the busiest cruise and cargo port in the Caribbean, 10th busiest in the U.S.
  • MICE growth: a modern convention center district overlooking the Port of San Juan now draws meetings, conferences, and exhibitions.

DEMOGRAPHICS

A century of growth, two decades of exodus

Population climbed for a hundred years, peaked at the 2000 census, then fell 11.8% in the 2010s alone as the debt crisis and back-to-back hurricanes pushed families to the mainland. More Puerto Ricans now live in the 50 states than on the island.

IDENTITY

How Puerto Ricans describe themselves changed overnight

Between the 2010 and 2020 censuses, "White alone" collapsed from 75.8% to 17.1% while "two or more races" jumped from 11.1% to 49.8% — less a demographic shift than a rethinking of identity on the form itself. 98.9% identify as Hispanic or Latino, and a 2003 DNA study found Indigenous maternal ancestry in 61% of islanders.

In 2010, White alone was 75.8% and Mixed 11.1% — the 2020 questionnaire redesign and shifting self-perception drove one of the largest identity swings ever recorded in a U.S. census.

GOVERNMENT & STATUS

Self-governing, but not sovereign

  • Republican structure on the American model: an elected governor (currently Jenniffer González-Colón), a bicameral Legislative Assembly, and a Supreme Court.
  • Congress holds plenary power under the Territorial Clause. Residents send one nonvoting resident commissioner to the U.S. House and cannot vote in presidential elections.
  • Taxes are real but partial: islanders pay Social Security, Medicare, and local income tax, while most personal income earned on-island is exempt from federal income tax.
  • Three parties, three futures: the PPD defends the commonwealth, the PNP seeks statehood, and the PIP seeks independence.
  • The status question stays open in San Juan, Washington, and the United Nations, which has repeatedly urged a self-determination process.

THE STATUS DEBATE

Statehood keeps winning the ballot, and going nowhere

Four plebiscites this century have favored statehood, though boycotts and turnout disputes cloud each result. The Puerto Rico Status Act passed the U.S. House in 2022 but died in the Senate; in November 2024 voters chose statehood again.

ECONOMY

Pharma island

  • Manufacturing leads the economy — pharmaceuticals above all. Puerto Rico has the second-largest pharmaceutical manufacturing output in the United States, behind only Indiana.
  • Services follow: finance, insurance, real estate, and tourism, which supplies up to 10% of GDP. Agriculture is under 1% of GNP.
  • High income, uneven prosperity: the highest GDP per capita in Latin America, yet median household income of $27,213 (2024) versus $81,604 nationally, with 37% below the poverty line.
  • The debt crisis: $70B+ in bond debt triggered the largest bankruptcy-type case in U.S. public bond history under PROMESA's oversight board (2016–).
  • The Jones Act requires U.S.-flagged ships between American ports, a century-old rule the island has repeatedly asked Congress to lift; studies disagree on its true cost.

CULTURE

Taíno drum, Spanish string, global stage

Music

From bomba, plena, and danza to salsa, reggaeton, and Latin trap. The jíbaro-built cuatro is the national instrument; Bad Bunny, Ricky Martin, and Menudo carried island sounds worldwide, and tenor Antonio Paoli recorded the first complete opera in history (1907).

Art & craft

Horned vejigante carnival masks of papier-mâché and coconut shell; carved santos that carried prayers between earth and heaven; Rococo painter José Campeche and a literary line running from Hostos to the Nuyorican movement.

Cuisine

Spanish, African, and Taíno roots on one plate: mofongo, pasteles, lechón, and arroz con gandules, the national dish. The piña colada was born here, and flan and tembleque close the meal.

SPORTS

A flag of its own on the world stage

The single-starred flag flies separately at the Olympics — Wikimedia Commons
  • Baseball royalty: Hall of Famers Roberto Clemente, Orlando Cepeda, and Roberto Alomar; fourteen Caribbean Series titles and deep World Baseball Classic runs.
  • Boxing capital per capita: the global leader in world champions per person — Cotto, Trinidad, Benítez, Gómez.
  • Basketball history: in Athens 2004, Puerto Rico beat the USA 92–73 — the first team ever to defeat the Americans with NBA players in an Olympic tournament.
  • Ten Olympic medals, competing under its own flag since 1948. Mónica Puig won the first gold in Rio 2016, in women's tennis.
