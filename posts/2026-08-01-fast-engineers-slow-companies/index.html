Fast engineers, slow companies

AI coding gains are not showing up in company results. Individual engineers are visibly faster. Company output looks about the same. A few explanations hold up, so I am writing them down.

Organizational drag

Faster workers inside slow systems still produce slow companies. Writing code was one step in a long cycle: planning, review, approvals, deploys, coordination. If writing code was 20% of cycle time, making that step five times faster barely moves the total. The bottleneck moved. It did not disappear.

The multiplicative problem

Value is multiplicative along a chain. Any zero along the path destroys the whole thing. Speeding up one link does nothing if a different link is the zero. The practical implication is to work as close to the outcome as possible, where fewer links sit between your work and the result.

Adoption is one guy

Even fifty-person orgs often have exactly one person on AI-powered hyper productivity while everyone else works as before. Non-technical folks mostly have not gone deep enough on the tools to get real leverage. And many orgs push adoption onto subject matter experts who are afraid their knowledge will get automated, so they never call the results good enough.

Review is the new bottleneck

Teams are drowning in PRs. Generation got cheap. Verification did not. A team that can produce ten times the pull requests but review at the same rate has just moved the queue.

Measurement was already broken

Engineering productivity was hard to measure before AI, and estimation makes even less sense now. Sales cannot guesstimate promises, managers cannot build schedules, and developers are working in a haze. Code was always a means to an end. Tracking it as the end was the original error, and AI just made the error visible.

Competition eats the gains

Everyone has access to the same models. Some gains get competed away instead of showing up as margin. Others will take longer to land in results. And the outside world can only absorb so many new features at once.

My take

This matches what I see. The constraint moved from writing to deciding, reviewing, and coordinating. The interesting agent work right now is on those surfaces, review queues and shared workspaces, rather than on faster code generation. One person with one AI and a clear head can outrun a team that has to stop and coordinate, but a company is the team, not the person.
