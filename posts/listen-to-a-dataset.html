A dataset you can listen to

AI music models don't train on sound. They train on tables. This page pulls a live row from a Hugging Face dataset of 422,000+ piano pieces, where each row is just a list of numbers, and turns that row back into music in your browser. Type any row number and press play.

The dataset

Itsuki-music/text2music-generation-data-collection on Hugging Face ↗

It's a table of solo piano pieces built to train a text-to-music model. One row per piece, three columns. The music lives entirely in the third column.

columntypewhat it holds
piece_idstringname of the piece, e.g. sample_000000
n_tokensint32how many numbers encode this piece
input_idslist<int32>the piece itself, as a sequence of integers

Three sample rows, with input_ids truncated:

The rows come in three stages of data quality:

stagepieceswhere the music came from
weak371,053Aria-MIDI v1: piano performances transcribed from public recordings, built by EleutherAI
middle51,130named public-domain piano recordings, transcribed from audio
lyria9,999solo piano audio generated by Lyria (Google's music model), transcribed to MIDI

Source and credit

Published on Hugging Face by Itsuki-music (August 2026) as the training data release for their piano generation project. So none of this music was composed by hand for the dataset: the weak and middle stages are real human performances that were transcribed from audio recordings into MIDI, and the lyria stage is model-generated audio transcribed the same way. The weak stage derives from EleutherAI's Aria-MIDI dataset. License is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (free to use and share with credit, no commercial use), inherited from Aria-MIDI. The repo also ships the raw MIDI files and the tokenizer that produced the integers, which is what makes this page possible.

How a row becomes music

Every piece in this dataset was flattened into one long list of integers so a language model can read it the same way it reads text. Three kinds of numbers do almost all the work:

1. The row: raw numbers

What's actually stored. This is the start of the row you loaded.

—
→

2. Decoded: musical events

Each number maps to a meaning: which key was pressed, how hard when for how long. Header numbers key, tempo, structure describe the whole piece.

—
→

3. Sound

With press + when + how long for every note, the piece is fully reconstructed. Your browser synthesizes each press with a simple piano tone. That's what the play button does.

—

The point worth sitting with: the entire performance survives the trip through a spreadsheet. Time becomes a column, pitch becomes a column, touch becomes a column, and nothing musical is lost. A model that learns to predict the next number in these rows is, by construction, learning to play the piano.
