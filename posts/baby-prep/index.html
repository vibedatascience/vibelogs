Baby prep

Baby due November 17. This page is the whole plan: what to buy, why each pick won, and the schedule everything hangs on. It started as two pages of clipboard notes and got the same treatment I give a work project. Find the dependencies, find the deadlines, sort by what blocks what.

The framework

Three numbers drive everything. The due date is November 17. Full term is 37 weeks, which lands on October 27, and after that the baby can arrive on any day it likes. So the real deadline is October 20, one week of margin at 36 weeks. The baby shower is October 10, and it matters for planning because you should not buy anything big before it and you have only ten days to fill the gaps after it.

Every task then sorts into one of four buckets:

  1. Long lead time. Registry, shower invitations, furniture shipping, doula booking, leave paperwork. These eat the calendar even though the baby is months away.
  2. Hospital critical. Car seat, hospital bag, a place for the baby to sleep. The hospital will not release the baby without a car seat.
  3. Zero lead time. Diapers, wipes, creams, bottles. Next day shipping exists. Buying these early just fills the apartment.
  4. Not actually needed yet. High chair at month five, toys at month three, babyproofing at month six. Deferring these is a decision, and it feels great.
GOTCHA · REGISTRY ORDER
The registry has to exist before the invitations go out, and the invitations set the shower, and the shower decides what you still have to buy. Get the registry wrong and the whole chain slips. It is the single biggest dependency node in the plan.

GOTCHA · SECONDHAND
The registry has a secondhand toggle per item. It stays off for exactly one category: the car seat. A used seat carries an unknown crash history and an expiration date, and there is no way to verify either. Everything else, used is fine.

How the car seat got picked

The first pass at this decision had nine dimensions. Then the no car fact collapsed it to three.

  1. Carrier weight. Seat plus baby is what actually gets lifted, through the lobby, twice per trip, for a year. A 6 lb seat against a 9 lb seat is a real difference at that frequency.
  2. Baseless install. Every car ride is a hired car, so the seat has to install safely with just a seatbelt, fast, on a curb. The feature name to search for is European belt routing.
  3. Stroller click. Curb to apartment should be one motion, seat snapping onto the frame, no adapters in a bag somewhere.

The Aria wins all three. Most of the comparison shopping people do on car seats is between models that differ on dimensions that stop mattering the moment you do not own a car.

GOTCHA · THE CRUNCH
Shower to fully ready is ten days. That window only works if nothing hospital critical is left to chance in it, which is why the plan pre buys the car seat the week before if nobody has gifted it. Consumables can ship overnight. A car seat stuck in transit cannot be argued with.

What fell off the list

The clipboard version had a high chair, toys, and a baby camera near the top. The high chair moved to month five, when solids start. Toys moved to month three, because a newborn does not play and gifts will cover it anyway. The camera survives as an open decision between a dumb local monitor and an app one that logs sleep data. I track everything else in my life, so the app one is probably inevitable.
