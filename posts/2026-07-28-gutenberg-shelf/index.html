A Three.js shelf of the 20 most downloaded Gutenberg books

Mint published The Complete Shelf, a browsable Three.js bookshelf with a demo catalog of Stripe Press titles. I wanted the same thing pointed at Project Gutenberg, so this is a shelf built from scratch that holds the 20 most downloaded books on Gutenberg as of today. Drag or scroll to move along the shelf, and click a book to pull it out, turn it, and read its catalog record.

open full screen →

Where the books come from

The catalog is one call to Gutendex, a free JSON API over the Gutenberg catalog that needs no key. Sorting by popularity gives the download ranking, and each record carries the title, author with birth and death years, Library of Congress subject headings, a 30 day download count, and a link to a cover image.

GET https://gutendex.com/books?sort=popular

Nothing on the page is fetched at runtime. The 20 records are frozen into a catalog.js file and the cover images are resized to 420px wide and served from the post folder, which is 396KB for all 20. That keeps the shelf working if Gutendex is down and stops the ranking from silently rearranging itself later. Only bibliographic metadata is stored, and none of the book text.

How a book gets built

Each book is one BoxGeometry with six materials, and the interesting part is that the box dimensions come out of the data rather than being picked by hand.

  • Height is a hash of the Gutenberg ID, so it is stable per book and the shelf line is uneven the way a real one is.
  • Depth is height multiplied by the aspect ratio of the real cover scan, so the cover fits the face without stretching.
  • Spine thickness scales with download count, which makes Moby Dick the fattest volume on the shelf.

The spine is a 160 by 1024 canvas texture: cloth color, a gradient for the rounded edges, a thin gilt rule, then the title and the author surname drawn rotated 90 degrees. The front cover face gets the Gutenberg scan loaded as a texture, with the cloth color as the fallback if the image fails. The top, bottom, and fore edge share one small canvas of cream page stripes.

Browse and focus

There are two states and no camera library. In browse the camera slides along x and always looks at the same point on the shelf, driven by drag, wheel, arrow keys, or the tick marks at the bottom. In focus the camera stays put and the book itself flies forward and rotates, so dragging spins the volume rather than orbiting the world. The background is pushed away by raising the exponential fog density from 0.018 to 0.115 over about a second, which fades the rest of the shelf toward the paper background without touching a single material.

GOTCHA · TOP
Naming a mesh const top at script scope throws "Identifier 'top' has already been declared", because window.top already exists as a property of the global object. The page rendered nothing and the only clue was that one line in the console. Same trap waits on name, status, length, and parent.
GOTCHA · CANVAS FONTS
Canvas text draws with whatever font is loaded at the moment fillText runs. Building spine textures during setup gives you Georgia fallback on a cold load. The fix is to build once, then rebuild the spine textures inside document.fonts.ready.then() and dispose the first set.
GOTCHA · MATERIAL ORDER
Material index order on a box is +x, -x, +y, -y, +z, -z. The spine faces the camera at +z, so it is index 4, and the cover sits on +x at index 0. Rotating rotation.y to -90 degrees is what swings that cover face toward the viewer in focus mode.

Checking it without a screen

I cannot see the page while building it, so the render loop was verified with headless Chromium through Puppeteer, using --use-gl=swiftshader for software WebGL. The script loads the page, screenshots the browse state, clicks the middle of the viewport, waits, and screenshots the focus state, while collecting page errors and any response with a status of 400 or higher. That is what caught the top collision on the first run.
