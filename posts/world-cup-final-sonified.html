What a World Cup final sounds like

This page plays the 2022 World Cup final (Argentina 3-3 France, penalties) as a piece of music. Every note is a real event from StatsBomb's data: passes become notes whose pitch rises as the ball moves toward goal, long carries become bass drones, shots ring out as loud as their xG, and goals land as chords. Argentina plays piano, France plays electric guitar. The match runs at 30x speed, so 128 minutes of football become about 5 minutes of sound. Press play and listen for the 79th minute.

The mapping

Sonification fails when everything makes sound. So only three event types play, and each carries the two or three numbers that matter:

eventsounddriven by
Pass (1,263 of them)one note, team's instrumentpitch from field position (own goal = low, opponent goal = high, snapped to a minor pentatonic scale), loudness from pass length, note length from actual pass duration
Carry over 1.2s (~450)sustained bass noteheld for the carry's real duration; the sound of a team keeping the ball
Shot (38)loud high strikeloudness from xG, so a big chance hits hard even when it misses; goals add a full chord and a marker on the timeline

Everything else (pressures, duels, throw-ins) stays silent on purpose. Timing is the match's real timing, compressed: a quick one-two sounds like a quick one-two. Halftime and the breaks before extra time are short rests. Left and right wing map to stereo left and right if panning is on.

What to listen for

France's first 70 minutes are sparse guitar over Argentina's steady piano, which is the sound of 60% possession. Minutes 79-81 are the loudest 4 seconds of the piece: two France goals 97 match-seconds apart. Extra time gets frantic in both instruments, and the shootout at the end is five isolated strikes per side with nothing in between.
