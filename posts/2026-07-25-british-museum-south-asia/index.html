A scroll flight through the British Museum, South Asia

Same scroll-flight engine as the scroll worlds post, pointed at a building this time. Scrolling walks one continuous camera from the Great Court, through the north door, and down the South Asia galleries in Room 33 and 33a. There are no cuts. The whole museum here is procedural three.js geometry lit like the real galleries: daylight through the glass roof, then dark rooms with one spotlight per object.

open full screen →

The route makes six stops. The Great Court under the Foster roof, the Amaravati Great Stupa drum slab, the Buddhapada footprint panel, the Shiva Nataraja from Tamil Nadu, the gilded Tara from Sri Lanka, and a climb back out through the glass. Every fact on the cards comes from the museum's own printed visitor guide, which I had open on my lap while building this: dates, heights, room numbers, findspots.

What carried over from the skill

  1. One monotonic camera path. The galleries sit on a straight line going north, so the camera never reverses; it weaves left and right between plinths instead.
  2. Smoothed camera, raw UI. Cards and rail dots read the raw scroll fraction, so headless QA can assert every band at any frame rate.
  3. Objects are stylized, not scanned. The Nataraja is a torus, cones, and cylinders under bronze metalness; the Tara is a lathe profile under gold. With one warm spotlight each in a dark room, silhouette and material do the work.
  4. Carvings are canvas textures. The stupa relief and the Buddhapada are drawn procedurally to a 2d canvas and used as both color map and bump map on plain boxes.
GOTCHA · REALISM BUDGET
Photoreal museum interiors are an asset problem, not a rendering problem. The move that works in one self-contained file is picking objects whose identity survives simplification: a ring of fire, a pair of carved footprints, a gold silhouette. Architecture with strong geometry (the Great Court roof) reads as itself; sculpture reads through material and lighting.
