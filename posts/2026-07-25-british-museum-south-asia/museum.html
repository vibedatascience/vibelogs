Room 33a · Amaravati · 300 BC to AD 400

The Great Stupa

Amaravati in south-east India was among the most important Buddhist sites in the country, in continual use for over 1000 years. This limestone drum slab carries a carving of the Great Stupa itself, which helps reconstruct where the surviving reliefs once sat.

Room 33a · Amaravati

Pair of Buddhapada

In the early period of Buddhist sculpture in India the Buddha was not shown in human form. He was depicted through symbols instead, and this limestone panel carries the Buddhapada, the Buddha's footprints.

Room 33 · Tamil Nadu · around AD 1100

Shiva Nataraja

Shiva as Lord of the Dance, turning inside a ring of fire. Nestling in his hair is a small figure of the goddess Ganga, the personification of the Ganges. The Nataraja is one of the best known images in Indian art, carried through temple festivals on parade.

Room 33 · Sri Lanka · around AD 700s

Tara

A gilded bronze of the Buddhist goddess Tara, found on the east coast of Sri Lanka near Trincomalee. One of the finest bronzes to survive from the island, evidence of the presence of Mahayana Buddhism there.
