A training week as one card

I wanted to lose the belly and get properly fit, so I worked out a week of training in a chat and then hit the real problem. A chat log is useless in a gym. You cannot scroll back three screens between sets to find out how many reps you are meant to do. So I asked for the same plan as a single page I could hold in one hand.

open full screen →

Tap the day strip to move through the week. Tap the circles as you finish each set and the bar under the header fills up. Every exercise carries a plain description of the movement and a link that opens a video search, since a still photo does not show you what a lift actually looks like.

The week

Four lifting sessions, one class, one easy cardio day, one walking day. Upper and lower body twice each. A four day split hits everything twice and survives a missed day far better than a five day one does.

The reasoning that took the longest was the easy cardio. It is the piece most people drop because it feels like nothing is happening, and it is the piece that builds the base everything else sits on.

Built for the gym that exists

The first draft assumed a squat rack and a straight barbell, because that is what a default program assumes. Then I photographed the gym and sent the pictures back. There is no rack and no straight bar. What there is: dumbbells from 10 up to 65 pounds in small steps, a cable machine with a lat pulldown and a rope, flat and adjustable benches, a set of fixed weight EZ curl bars, and cardio machines.

So every lift got rebuilt around that. Back squats became goblet squats, deadlifts became dumbbell stiff leg deadlifts, the leg press became walking lunges and step ups. Very little is actually lost. For a first twelve weeks dumbbells are arguably the better tool, because each side has to work on its own and there is no loaded bar to get stuck under when a set goes badly.

GOTCHA · EQUIPMENT
The fixed EZ curl bars are curved and short. They will not sit flat across your upper back and the weight is fixed, so they are useless for squats no matter how much they look like a barbell. They are for curls.

The card now carries a suggested starting weight next to every exercise, which removes the one decision that stalls people on their first session. Those numbers come from the sizes actually on the rack in the photo.

Design

The whole page assumes you are standing up, holding a phone in one hand, slightly out of breath. Everything else follows from that. Headings are set in condensed type large enough to read at arm's length, each block fits roughly one screen, and the tap targets for the set counters are big enough to hit without looking carefully.

There is no build step and no framework. It is one HTML file with an inline style block and about sixty lines of JavaScript, same as everything else here.

GOTCHA · STATE
Set check marks live in a plain JavaScript object and reset when the tab closes. Persisting them would mean storage and a sync story, and I would rather find out whether I use this for two weeks before building that.

What the plan actually says

The training is the easy half. The five rules at the bottom of the card are the half that decides whether any of it works: log every weight, eat at a modest deficit rather than a punishing one, get protein at every meal, keep drinking down to a few a week with none on lifting days, and judge the scale on a weekly average instead of a daily number.

The deficit is set at roughly 400 calories under maintenance, not more. At a normal starting weight the belly is a body composition problem rather than a weight problem, and cutting harder mostly costs you muscle, which makes the waist read softer rather than tighter.
