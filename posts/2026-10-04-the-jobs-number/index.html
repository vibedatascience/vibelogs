01 / what it is

A headcount of paid jobs, minus last month's headcount

Every month BLS asks about 120,000 employers one question: how many people did you pay in the pay period that includes the 12th?

Why the 12th? Every employer reports the same mid-month moment, clear of month-end and big holidays, and it matches the week the household survey asks about. So the report is a snapshot of one week: a layoff on Sep 20 shows up in October's number.

03 / how to read the headline

Three comparisons do most of the work

September vs the forecast, recent averages, 2019, and the breakeven range.
  1. Forecast: 84k. The median guess of economists at banks and research firms, polled by news services like Bloomberg before the release (Bloomberg's poll had 90k). They expected more because August first came in at +162k. It was later cut to +133k.
  2. Breakeven: about 0 to 87k. The jobs needed each month to keep unemployment flat. Less immigration means fewer new workers, so the bar is low. 29k sits inside that range.
  3. Revisions: -60k. July and August were cut, and unemployment rose to 4.2%.
Verdict: hiring is slow but near the new normal, and unemployment is creeping up while the Fed raises rates.

What comes before a sustained rise?

The surprise itself barely predicts the next month. How the market closes on Friday does. Average S&P 500 path after each kind of Friday:

Watch Friday's close, not the headline. Stocks down with yields up is a rate scare, and it is the one pattern that kept sliding. Oct 2 was stocks up, yields down.

Why the sign flips. Strong jobs mean a stronger economy, which is good for profits, but also more pressure to keep rates high. When inflation is low the first effect wins. When inflation is above target, as in 2026, the second does, so a miss can lift stocks.

Expected = average of the prior three months, a stand-in for economists' forecasts, whose history is paywalled. The rough stand-in weakens every correlation. 2020-21 left out. 2026 is nine reports. Small groups (19 to 30 reports) in a mostly rising market, so treat these as tendencies, not rules.
